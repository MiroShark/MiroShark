# Gas Tax Public Explainer — 15-Slide Outline

**Purpose:** Turn the current 48-slide gas-tax curriculum into a concise public-facing education sequence that communicates the full story without overwhelming viewers.

**Target format:** TikTok/Reels/Shorts carousel or narrated vertical video.  
**Target length:** 90–150 seconds.  
**Audience:** General public; assumes no tax/budget knowledge.  
**Tone:** Calm, fair, explanatory, non-partisan.

## Core editorial promise

Do not tell people simply “tax gas more” or “don’t tax gas more.” Teach the system:

1. Gas is a public resource extracted by private firms.
2. The argument is about whether Australia captures a fair share of economic rent.
3. Government budgets are larger than this one tax debate.
4. More revenue does not automatically solve debt or services.
5. Policy design matters more than slogans.

## 15-slide sequence

### 1. Hook — “Should gas companies pay more tax?”
- **Source from current deck:** `opening.overview`
- **Public message:** This is not just a tax fight. It is a public-resource, private-profit, services, and investment-risk story.
- **Visual:** Gas field → company profits → public budget → trade-off scale.
- **Voiceover:** “Before we argue about a gas tax, we need to know what story we’re really telling.”

### 2. The surface claim
- **New/reworked slide needed**
- **Public message:** The public hears two simple claims: “gas companies should pay more” and “new taxes will scare investment.” Both hide context.
- **Visual:** Two megaphones facing each other: “Fair share” vs “Investment risk.”
- **Voiceover:** “Both sides use slogans. The useful question is: what is true, what is missing, and what trade-off are we accepting?”

### 3. What is being taxed?
- **New/reworked slide needed**
- **Public message:** A resource-rent tax is aimed at extra profits from publicly owned natural resources, not ordinary wages.
- **Visual:** Public underground resource → extraction company → ordinary costs/profits vs “resource rent.”
- **Voiceover:** “The strongest case for taxing gas is that the resource belongs to the public, and very high returns can include resource rent.”

### 4. What tax does Canberra already collect?
- **Source from current deck:** `money.tax_revenue_numbers`
- **Public message:** Most Commonwealth revenue comes from workers, company tax, GST, and other broad taxes. PRRT is tiny beside the big tax streams.
- **Visual:** Clean proportional revenue chart; avoid tiny unreadable labels.
- **Voiceover:** “Gas-specific tax is only a small piece of the federal revenue picture.”

### 5. Where does public money go?
- **Source from current deck:** `money.spending_numbers` + strongest breakdown cards
- **Public message:** Spending is dominated by welfare, health, education, defence, states, aged care, NDIS, and interest.
- **Visual:** Big buckets, not 10 tiny categories.
- **Voiceover:** “When people ask why government needs money, this is the starting map.”

### 6. The missing context: raw totals can mislead
- **Source from current deck:** `money.tax_per_person`
- **Public message:** Tax per resident rose from about $10.4k to $23.8k in nominal terms, but that average includes all taxes and must be read with inflation, wages, profits, and population.
- **Visual:** Population +31% beside tax/person 2.3× nominal, with a warning: “average across all taxes.”
- **Voiceover:** “Yes, nominal tax collected per resident rose. But that does not mean every person personally paid that amount.”

### 7. Where did the extra money come from?
- **Source from current deck:** `money.extra_sources`
- **Public message:** The extra revenue mostly came from a bigger economy and tax base: workers, companies, GST, super, and other receipts.
- **Visual:** 2006 receipts → 2024–25 receipts, with arrows for worker/company/GST/super growth.
- **Voiceover:** “A bigger government budget is mostly funded by a bigger tax base — more wages, profits, prices, people, and economic activity.”

### 8. Why can tax per person rise without a dramatic new tax?
- **Source from current deck:** `money.tax_pressure_reasons`
- **Public message:** Inflation, wage growth, bracket creep, company profits, service costs, and debt repair can all raise collected dollars.
- **Visual:** Central “more tax/person” node with six causes.
- **Voiceover:** “The system can collect more even when no single new tax explains the whole change.”

### 9. If revenue rose, why isn’t debt gone?
- **Source from current deck:** `debt.why_not_paid_down`
- **Public message:** Debt falls only when revenue exceeds spending and interest by enough, for long enough.
- **Visual:** Leaky bucket: revenue in; services, interest, promises out; surplus pays debt.
- **Voiceover:** “More revenue is not the same as surplus. Debt only falls when there is money left after spending and interest.”

### 10. So where does gas tax fit?
- **Source from current deck:** `debt.unwind_options` + new framing
- **Public message:** A gas tax is one possible revenue source, not a full budget or debt solution.
- **Visual:** Debt repair choices: grow, spend less, tax more, mix; gas tax is one branch under “tax more.”
- **Voiceover:** “A gas tax may help revenue, but it cannot carry the whole budget story alone.”

### 11. The strongest case for taxing more
- **Source from current deck:** `claims.supporters_case`, `precedents.success_cases`
- **Public message:** Supporters argue Australia should capture more value from public resources and use it for services, debt repair, or future generations.
- **Visual:** Public resource → fair return → citizens/services/future fund.
- **Voiceover:** “The strongest pro-tax argument is not envy. It is that public resources should deliver a fair public return.”

### 12. The strongest case against taxing more
- **Source from current deck:** `claims.opponents_case`, `precedents.backfire_cases`
- **Public message:** Opponents warn that sudden or poorly designed taxes can reduce investment, supply, jobs, or trust in policy rules.
- **Visual:** Tax shock → investor concern → supply/jobs warnings.
- **Voiceover:** “The strongest anti-tax argument is not that companies should never pay more. It is that bad design can create real economic costs.”

### 13. The key design questions
- **New/reworked slide needed**
- **Public message:** The real debate is design: rate, timing, deductions, price triggers, transition rules, where money goes, and transparency.
- **Visual:** Checklist labelled “Good tax design?”
- **Voiceover:** “Once you get past slogans, the debate becomes practical: what rate, what base, what safeguards, and what public benefit?”

### 14. What each side leaves out
- **Source from current deck:** `evidence.confidence_map`, `stakeholders.map`
- **Public message:** Industry often downplays public ownership/resource rent. Pro-tax campaigns can understate investment and design risks.
- **Visual:** Two columns: “Industry may omit…” / “Tax advocates may omit…”
- **Voiceover:** “A fair explanation should steelman both sides and show what each side prefers not to talk about.”

### 15. Conclusion — “The honest answer”
- **Source from current deck:** `conclusion.recommendation`, but rewrite needed
- **Public message:** The honest conclusion is not a slogan. More tax can be justified if it is evidence-based, stable, targeted at rents, transparent, and paired with a clear public benefit.
- **Visual:** Balanced bridge: fair public return ↔ investment certainty.
- **Voiceover:** “The public should not ask only ‘tax or no tax?’ The better question is: what design gives Australians a fair return without creating avoidable harm?”

## What to cut from the public version

These are useful in the master curriculum but too detailed for the first public explainer:

- Full government-by-government budget timeline slides.
- Deep dives on every spending category.
- Repeated precedent slides unless they contain concrete country examples.
- Any placeholder labels with ellipses or unclear generated text.
- Detailed debt blame/fault mapping unless the video is specifically about debt.

## What to add before publishing

1. **A clean definition slide for resource rent / PRRT / proposed windfall tax.**
2. **A concrete estimate slide:** “How much could the proposal raise?” with source and uncertainty.
3. **A source drawer or end-card:** official budget papers, ATO/PRRT data, ABS population/inflation, industry claims, tax-reform claims.
4. **A design checklist slide:** rate, base, deductions, transition, price trigger, revenue use.
5. **A final “how to think” card:** true / missing / contested / decision.
